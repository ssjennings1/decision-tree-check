# Research memo: getting owners to start and share the quiz, and how to launch it

Prepared 2026-10-27. Paraphrased; sources at the end. Honesty note first: almost every title and hook number here is Interact's own uncontrolled platform data or ScoreApp-ecosystem claims. The one peer-reviewed quiz-specific sharing study is Huber, Reinecke & Gajos (CHI 2017). Treat the direction as reliable and the percentages as indicative.

## What the evidence says to do for this quiz

1. **Headline is a second-person question. The framework name goes on the result, not the door.** Interact's analysis of its top 100 quizzes found about three-quarters used a question aimed at the reader ("Which X are you?", "How much do you really..."), and that "actually"/"really" in the title correlated with 17–25% more views. Nobody has published data showing a branded-framework title ("The X Method Check") beats a plain question; the practitioner consensus runs the other way. So the quiz is named by its question, and "Decision Tree" is what they discover inside and what the result is called.

2. **The landing page has one job: a start.** ScoreApp's model, echoed by Interact: headline question, one line of what you get, a button with the time promise, question count, a single face. No explanation of the model above the button. A single-person cover image gets measurably more visits (Interact, uncontrolled).

3. **Gate after the questions, before the full result. Never before question one.** Every practitioner source agrees. Interact's benchmark with the gate at the end: about 65% of starters finish, about 40% become leads.

4. **The result must be flattering-but-true and have a name.** Huber et al. (about 75,000 participants): people share self-assessment results that enhance them; the most positive framing of an identical score roughly tripled the share rate (from about 0.7% to about 2%). Interact's look at 2,000 shared results found about 75% contained positive words. Share rates are low in absolute terms, so sharing is a bonus, not the engine. Named tiers get shared; raw percentages mostly don't unless the number surprises.

5. **Launch sequence: list first, then a LinkedIn series, then evergreen.** Interact: email the existing list with a screenshot, one line and a button, to the quiz on your own site. LinkedIn: polls get about 1.8x the reach of a normal post on personal profiles (AuthoredUp), and a results-of-the-poll post with your read often beats the poll. Metricool's 2025 sample of 577k posts found posts with external links did slightly better, not worse, than posts without, so the "links kill reach" folklore is not supported there. Evergreen: site nav, newsletter footer, email signature, QR on talk slides and cards.

6. **What kills starts for an owner audience.** Practitioner consensus, not studies: gating before question one; questions that read as qualification; a page that explains the method before the button; a title that reads like an exam you can fail. Priestley's framing: questions should surface tension gently; blunt questions shut people down. Situations, not self-ratings.

## Title data in more detail

- Interact hook ranking (2024–25): identity, conversation, question, personalization, audience fit, concern, benefit, curiosity, immediacy, relevance. They warn that BuzzFeed tone doesn't fit every audience and recommend testing two titles.
- Interact recipes: assessment = "How [trait] are you?", "Are you ready to...?", "What's your [level]?"; personality = "What type of [X] are you?"
- ScoreApp convention: "The [Topic] Scorecard" as the asset name, but the landing hook is still a question. Naming convention, not test data.
- Naming the audience in the title: no data. Interact's advice is to speak to one person, not label a segment.

## Scorecard specifics (Priestley / ScoreApp, via playbooks; scoreapp.com itself was blocked)

Landing page = credibility, clarity, connection, one goal. 8–15 questions for cold traffic. Score out of 100 plus category breakdown. Three tiers with tailored content and a different CTA per tier (low: free resource; mid: discovery call; high: direct conversation). Results email immediately. Abandonment email. The "30–50% conversion vs. 3–10% for PDFs" claim comes from playbooks and is unsourced.
