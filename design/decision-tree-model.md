# The Decision Tree — canonical model

**Original graphic:** `design/decision-tree-original.png` (Shannon's own, shared 2026-10-27). Its definitions, which differ slightly from the Oct 26 Seed wording:

| Level | Original graphic | Oct 26 Seed wording |
|-------|------------------|---------------------|
| Leaf | Employees empowered to make these without taking the manager's time. Example: FedEx or UPS? | You decide. No need to tell me. |
| Branch | Employees empowered to make these and report what they've done to their manager. | You decide, then tell me. |
| Limb | Employee and supervisor make this decision together. | You decide, but talk to me before you act. |
| Trunk | Employee and supervisor involve additional teams/leaders, because it reaches beyond the functional area. | We decide together. |
| Root | Made by the whole organization; may involve key stakeholders. | I decide, with input from the people who'll live with it. |

The two agree on the shape (who gets involved widens as you go down the tree). They differ on Limb (together vs. talk first) and Root (whole organization vs. owner with input). Which one governs is Shannon's call; the tool's item placements are checked against both below.

Source: Shannon's own teaching, latest wording from the Saturday Seed welcome sequence Email 2 (Oct 26, 2026) and Field Note 1. Credit line: *"The Decision Tree started years ago from Susan Scott's work at Fierce. It's grown a lot in my hands since."*

The tree is just one metaphor. Build one that works for you. The idea is clarity around responsibility, decision-making, and communication: everyone knows what they can decide on their own, what they need to report back on, and what they'd better not do alone.

| Level | Rule (from the owner's chair) | Examples Shannon uses |
|-------|------------------------------|-----------------------|
| **Leaf** | You decide. No need to tell me. Low risk, high volume. | Which vendor for everyday shipping. A standard customer service fix. |
| **Branch** | You decide, then tell me. I trust you, I just want to know. | A customer escalation. Reviewing a vendor's performance. |
| **Limb** | You decide, but talk to me before you act. | A pricing exception. Moving someone to a new role. |
| **Trunk** | We decide together. Direction, risk, strategy. Shapes everything else. | A new service line. A major equipment purchase. |
| **Root** | I decide, with input from the people who'll live with it, and sometimes from outside. Make-or-break. | Selling, succession, a partner, a second location. |

**The goal of the tree (Shannon, 2026-10-27):** clarity, empowerment, development, and a leader's desk that holds the most important on-the-business items. Every result, One Move, and email should point at those four, not just at relief for the owner.

**The pattern the quiz exposes:** Most growing businesses treat everything like a trunk or root decision. Everything comes to the owner. The tree gets weighed down because nothing moves without the person at the center.

**The story that goes with it:** The owner who built a tree on the wall outside her office. Every decision her people were empowered to make got a leaf with the key information and the person's name on it. People started stopping at the tree instead of knocking. The line got shorter. (Field Note 1 / Seed welcome Email 2.)

**Why five levels, not four:** Shannon keeps five because Scott's four is too simple. In practice root decisions are the owner's to make, with input.

**What the quiz does NOT do:** It does not teach the whole model or replace the room. The One Move is solo and reflective. The facilitation and coaching is where Shannon is the value.
