# Launch plan — What Landed on Your Desk Last Week?

**The one-line strategy:** This is referral infrastructure that happens to be a quiz. Its first job is to be the thing a CPA, a past client, or a peer-group chair can forward in one line. Its second job is a low-threat first step for a stranger. LinkedIn is third.

Why that order (from `research/`): 55–80% of clients like Shannon's come from referrals; 72% of buyers never referred anyone because nobody asked; referrers judge expertise by hearing her speak and reading her. LinkedIn organic reach fell 34–50% in 2025. Scorecard cases that cite revenue tied the launch to a dated event.

**Nothing in this plan goes live without Shannon's yes. It's a complete plan, ready to run.**

Taft gives start/finish/opt-in from quiz stats and tags. Calls come from the Clarity Call calendar with a "desk" source tag (add a hidden field or UTM on the booking link).

## Kill criteria

If after six weeks start → finish is under 50% AND opt-in is under 25%, the quiz is the problem, not the traffic. Cut to seven questions, rewrite the ones with the most drop-off, relaunch to the list only. If completion is fine but opt-in is low, the opt-in page copy is the problem. If both are fine and calls are zero, the result-page CTA is the problem; try a per-tier CTA (Shade Tree → Seed only; Switchboard → call; Builder → personal note first, call second).

## Version two (only if version one earns it)

The lead-gen report's "Owner Readiness Check": add dimensions (the bench behind you, the conversations nobody's had, the next downturn). Same builder, same tags, same workflow; add categories and questions. Don't build it until fifty real results say the narrow one works.

## What stays human

Shannon sends every partner email, every Builder note, and every reverse referral herself. The workflow sends result emails and the three-day follow-up. No AI replies to anyone.
