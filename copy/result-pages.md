# Result pages

Same shape every time: the name, the number, the heaviest branch, what it looks like from inside, what the desk is for, One Move for Monday, two buttons. The tree's goal runs through all three: clarity for everyone, people who are empowered and growing, and an owner's desk that holds the on-the-business work. Phone-first. No em dashes in body copy.

## Result A (0–12) — The Shade Tree

**Desk Load: {{quiz_tags.overall_score}} of 40.**

Most of the small stuff never reaches you, and the people handling it know when to loop you in. People work under your tree, not around it.

That didn't happen by accident. Somewhere along the way you drew the lines, or your people figured them out and you let them. Everybody knows what's theirs. What it buys you: the decisions that do reach your desk are mostly the ones that should. Direction. Where the next hire goes. The customer you're not set up for yet. The calls that shape everything else.

**One Move for Monday.** Find the one leaf that still climbs. Pick one decision in your heaviest branch this week and ask the person who brought it, "What would you do?" Then let them. That question is how people grow into the next branch.

The next stage of growth puts new weight on the tree. When you want a second set of eyes on where the lines should move, that's a conversation I'm glad to have.

## Result B (13–26) — The Switchboard

**Desk Load: {{quiz_tags.overall_score}} of 40.**

A good share of the everyday calls still route through you, and a good share don't. You're the connection point. Everybody knows that if they plug into you, they'll get an answer.

That's how a company grows when the owner is good at the work. People bring you things because you have the answer, and because they're not sure they're allowed to decide. From your chair it looks like interruptions. From theirs it looks like respect, or caution. Either way, nobody's quite sure where the lines are. The tree can't carry more growth until some of that weight moves down, and your people can't grow into the next branch until they know it's theirs. What that frees your desk for: the next hire, the next customer, the next year.

**One Move for Monday.** Look at the last five things that landed on your desk. Pick one from your heaviest branch, hand it back to the person it belongs to, and give them the new rule: you decide, then tell me. Say it out loud. Clarity is the gift.

If you'd rather draw the lines with your whole management team instead of one decision at a time, that's the kind of room I hold.

## Result C (27–40) — The Builder

**Desk Load: {{quiz_tags.overall_score}} of 40.**

Nearly everything on the list came to you, or waited for you. That's because you built this. You were the answer to every question in the building, and that's exactly how it grew.

It's also why you can't step away without things slowing down, and why you're the last to hear about the things that matter. Nobody did this to you. It's what happens when a company outgrows one person's ability to see everything and nobody has drawn the lines yet. The good news: builders are good at drawing lines. You just haven't pointed that at this yet. And the people bringing you things are ready for more than they're getting. Every decision you keep is one they don't get to grow on. Every one you hand back with a clear rule clears your desk for the work only you can do: where this company goes next.

**One Move for Monday.** Pick the one decision that came to you this week that you already knew the answer to before they finished asking. Hand it back with a rule attached: "This is yours. Make the call and tell me after." Then do it again next week. Watch who steps up.

When you're ready to draw the lines for the whole tree, with your people in the room, that's the work I do. Fifteen minutes is enough to see if it fits.
